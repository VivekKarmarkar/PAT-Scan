  The paper is SILENT on this question. It does not explicitly state whether the applied force is purely normal (perpendicular to the tissue surface) or whether tangential components are present, nor does it say which channels of the 6-axis F/T sensor are used by the inference pipeline.

  What the paper DOES say that's closest:

  1. Page 4, Eq. 6 (III-C-1) — the Hertzian + viscoelastic contact model uses only penetration depth d and its time derivative ḋ:

        FTOT(d, ḋ) = (4/3) · (Ef/(1−ν²)) · √(Rd) · d + (4/(1−ν)) · η · √(Rd) · ḋ

     This is a scalar equation in ONE force component — by construction, the normal direction (the axis of indentation). By adopting this model, the paper IMPLICITLY treats the relevant force as purely normal. But this is a claim about the INFERENCE model, not about the physical loading. Tangential forces, if present at the F/T sensor, are simply not consumed by the filter.
